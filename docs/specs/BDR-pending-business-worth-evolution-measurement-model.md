## 1. The Business Reality — Why This BDR Exists

Sabush BPT's central promise, per `02-business-worth-engine.md`, is answering "what is my business worth" from data the Admin is already recording — no separate bookkeeping exercise. Two real gaps stand between that promise and the platform today, both confirmed directly against the current implementation:

**Gap 1 — a first stock purchase is treated as if it establishes Business Worth, and it does not.** A business may spend weeks or months preparing and operating — purchasing stock, incurring expenses, investing in setup — before the owner is ready to say "this business can stand on its own." Today, the platform has no concept of that in-between period: it either shows an unset-state Dashboard card or, once an `initial` Stock Count exists, immediately treats that count's cost valuation as the permanent Initial Capital baseline (`10-stock-counts.md`, "Initial Capital — Architecture's strictest immutability tier"). This BDR establishes that a first purchase is not the same event as the business standing on its own, and the system must be able to represent the difference.

**Gap 2 — the platform has no concept of a *measured, dated series* of Business Worth snapshots, an owner-declared "the business can stand on its own" moment, or a narrow set of financial-position inputs (cash, receivables, payables) that Architecture's boundary today explicitly excludes.** `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s `businessWorth` formula is a single, always-current, live-computed figure — never itself a dated, immutable, drillable historical record the way a Closing snapshot is. Contagem (`10-stock-counts.md`) exists today as a physical-count and comparison mechanism, deliberately kept *outside* the Business Worth formula. This BDR settles that, under the new model, a confirmed Contagem becomes the mechanism that produces a dated Business Worth measurement (§4, Decision 1–4), while every existing historical record and the existing Dashboard itself are left exactly as they are (§4, Decisions 25–27). **[Amended, 23 August 2026, Decision 36]** Contagem is no longer the sole establishment mechanism — Owner-Declared Business Worth (§4, Decision 36, below) is a second, equally legitimate establishment method. This amendment does not reopen or weaken the treatment of every existing historical record and the existing Dashboard described above.

## 2. Conceptual Vocabulary — Fixed Terms for This Decision and Everything Built From It

The following terms are used exactly as defined here throughout this BDR and everything drafted from it. Several are the platform's own existing, real terms (confirmed directly in code/UI, not invented by this BDR); a few are genuinely new concepts this BDR introduces.

- **Contagem** — the platform's existing physical/financial stock-counting mechanism (`10-stock-counts.md`; UI label "Contagem" in `PeriodicStockCountView.tsx`). Unchanged in its existing mechanical shape: a date, a type, a list of counted items, quantities preserved exactly as entered. This BDR changes what a *confirmed* Contagem *means* under the new model (§4, Decisions 1–4), not what a Contagem *is* mechanically. Under the new model, Contagem is also the platform's **transition event** — the very first Contagem a business ever confirms is both what today's Initial Stock/Capital Inicial concept represents and the moment Business Worth transitions from UNKNOWN to measured (§4, Decision 1).
- **Contagem as measurement and reconciliation, not a purchase** — a confirmed Contagem records what the business physically/financially has *now*, and reconciles that physical reality against the system's own recorded financial reality (cash, and analogously stock quantities). It is never proof that counted products were purchased recently, and it is never treated as, or confused with, the amount originally invested (§4, Decisions 2, 11).
- **Business Worth (current usage)** — `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s existing formula (`totalMarketValueAllTime − totalExpensesAllTime − totalWithdrawalsAllTime`). This BDR does not delete or redefine that formula's own mechanics; it establishes that, going forward, Current Business Worth and Estimated Business Worth are the figures the Dashboard's existing Business Worth presentation surfaces (§4, Decision 3; §5, item 2).
- **Current Business Worth (new concept)** — the most recent confirmed Contagem-derived Business Worth snapshot under the new model (§4, Decisions 1–4, 23).
- **Estimated Business Worth (new concept)** — the system's running projection of Business Worth *between* two confirmed Contagens under the new model, distinct from and never confused with Current Business Worth (§4, Decisions 3, 21).
- **Startup Investment (new concept)** — everything the owner spends establishing the business, from the beginning until the owner declares the business can stand on its own (§4, Decision 20).
- **Capital Inicial (existing term, historically preserved)** — the platform's existing, real UI/code term (`InitialStockCountView.tsx`, `PeriodicStockCountView.tsx`) for the current `initial` Stock Count's frozen cost/selling valuation, governed today by `10-stock-counts.md` and `BDR-0014`. This BDR does not rename, delete, or reinterpret any existing business's historical Capital Inicial value (§4, Decision 25). Conceptually, going forward, wherever Capital Inicial represented the business's frozen baseline figure, that conceptual role is filled by Current Business Worth (§4, Decisions 3, 27).
- **Levantamentos** — the platform's existing, real term (`ClosingView.tsx`, `DashboardView.tsx`) for Withdrawals (`09-withdrawals.md`). Used here exactly as the existing module already defines it; explicitly not an ordinary operating expense (§4, Decision 19).
- **Quebras** — the platform's existing, real term (`07-breakages.md`) for Breakages. Used here exactly as the existing module already defines it, including its existing valuation basis (§4, Decision 18).
- **Fecho** — the platform's existing, real UI term ("Fecho Mensal/Anual," `11-monthly-closings.md`) for a Closing. Used here exactly as the existing module already defines its structural role (freezing a period); §4 Decision 24 adds a specific new requirement on top of it (arbitrary date ranges).
- **Embedded Profit** — `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s existing, unchanged `marketValue − investmentValue` figure. This BDR restates, and depends on, the existing rule that Embedded Profit never implies a sale occurred and must never be double-counted on top of a measured stock valuation (§4, Decision 5).
- **Receivables (new, narrowly scoped concept)** — money customers owe the business, tracked separately from Business Worth until actually paid (§4, Decision 13).
- **Supplier obligations / Payables (new, narrowly scoped concept)** — money the business owes suppliers, originating from purchase records, with a Paid/Partially Paid/Unpaid lifecycle (§4, Decision 14).
- **Cash Ledger (new, narrowly scoped concept)** — a complete, recorded ledger of business cash inflows/outflows (not an arbitrary typed balance), contributing to Business Worth and reconcilable against physical cash at Contagem (§4, Decisions 12, 11).

## 3. Existing System Behavior — Grounded in Repository Inspection

Recorded here as-is, without reinterpretation, so the DECIDED business rules in §4 can be checked against real current state rather than an assumed one:

1. `02-business-worth-engine.md`: `businessWorth = totalMarketValueAllTime − totalExpensesAllTime − totalWithdrawalsAllTime`. No cash, receivable, or payable term exists in this formula today. `capitalGrowth = businessWorth − initialCapitalValue`, where `initialCapitalValue` is sourced from the `initial` Stock Count's frozen `totalValue` (cost-basis, or cost/selling per `BDR-0014`'s owner-chosen basis where that capability applies).
2. `10-stock-counts.md`: the very first Stock Count a business ever performs (`type: 'initial'`) becomes the permanent Initial Capital baseline — immutable, enforced at the Security Rules layer. Every count after that (Contagem/Periodic Stock Count) exists as a comparison figure — `expectedCurrentStockValue` (Confirmed Initial Capital + cost value of governed StockBatch inventory) — deliberately kept *outside* the `businessWorth` formula, never folded into it. A Contagem today is not itself a dated Business Worth snapshot; it is a drift-check comparison.
3. `BDR-0014`: Initial Stock (and, per its Decision 7, Contagem) already supports preserving both a cost-basis and a selling-basis valuation total per portion, and multiple portions of one product at different unit/price bases, correctly summed. The owner may choose which basis "Initial Capital" displays; that choice is prospective-only and fixed once set. `BDR-0014` explicitly does not redefine how Initial Stock, StockBatch inventory, expenses, withdrawals, or realized profit ultimately combine into a single Business Worth figure (`BDR-0014` §7) — leaving exactly the question this BDR now answers.
4. `01-dashboard.md`: today's Dashboard renders a primary grid including a separate **Initial Capital card** and a separate **Business Worth card** side by side, plus a capital-growth badge on the Business Worth card. There is no single card today that itself opens a drillable history of dated snapshots.
5. `09-withdrawals.md` (Levantamentos) and `08-expenses.md`: both already reduce `businessWorth` directly in the existing formula, are structurally separate collections from each other, and are already reported in Fecho/Closing (`11-monthly-closings.md`).
6. `07-breakages.md` (Quebras): already reduces Investment Value and Market Value simultaneously (never one without the other), valued at cost (`quebraValue = quantityLost × costPrice`), and never adds Embedded Profit.
7. `11-monthly-closings.md`: Fecho today supports exactly two period types — `monthly` and `yearly` — plus a guard preventing the same exact period from being closed twice. There is no arbitrary-date-range Fecho mechanism today.
8. `BDR-0012`: Product Memory already remembers a product's unit relationships and latest recorded selling price/unit across transactions, without overwriting a specific transaction's own historically recorded facts — the same non-overwriting discipline this BDR's cost/price-memory rule (§4, Decision 9) depends on and restates rather than re-derives.
9. No cash ledger, no receivable record, no payable/supplier-obligation-lifecycle record, and no "business can stand on its own" declaration exist anywhere in the current schema or UI. These are the genuinely new surfaces this BDR's Architecture Boundary Override (§4, Decision 34) narrowly authorizes as future scope.

## 4. Decisions Formally Established (DECIDED — Product Architect Business Rules)

Each decision below was stated explicitly and unambiguously across the Product Architect's two consolidation sessions. None is inferred or guessed. Where a decision is a direct, obvious logical consequence of an explicitly stated rule rather than a verbatim restatement, it is marked **(logical consequence, converted to a formal rule)** rather than presented as a new open question. Numbering follows the settled-decisions numbering used in the final consolidation session, for direct traceability.

1. **When Business Worth exists.** A business does not automatically have Business Worth merely because it has purchased stock. A new business may spend a period buying products, preparing premises, buying materials, paying labor/transport/wages, and incurring other startup costs, generating activity over days or weeks — during which **Business Worth = UNKNOWN**. The system reports what is known instead: stock purchased, cost valuation, selling valuation, embedded profits, expenses, startup investment, other recorded activity. Business Worth begins when the owner decides "the business can stand on its own" and performs and confirms a Contagem — that first confirmed Contagem becomes the first measured Business Worth. **There is no separate "business is operational" button** unless later implementation analysis proves one necessary — the first confirmed Contagem is the transition event. **(Logical consequence, converted to a formal rule) — [CORRECTED, 23 August 2026, per the Governance Amendment Revision 3, §A.1]:** ~~because the platform's existing schema requires an `initial` Stock Count before any periodic Contagem exists, the first Contagem a business confirms under the new model *is* the same event today's Initial Stock/Capital Inicial confirmation already represents — this BDR does not introduce a second, parallel confirmation step; it reinterprets the meaning of that one existing confirmation event for businesses operating under the new model, without altering the confirmation mechanism itself.~~ **This premise was verified false by direct inspection of `firestore.rules` (the `stockCounts` create rule) and `AppContext.tsx`'s `recordStockCount`: a periodic Contagem has no dependency on an `initial` StockCount existing, and never has.** The corrected rule: a genuinely new business is never required to confirm Capital Inicial before its first Contagem. Capital Inicial (historical/startup capital information) and the first Contagem that establishes Business Worth are two independent, optional events — a new business may confirm Capital Inicial, may skip it entirely and proceed directly through `+Stock`/Startup Investment activity to its first Contagem, or may do both, in either order. For an existing business, its first Contagem under this model is never the same event as any historical Capital Inicial confirmation it may already have — the two remain entirely distinct at all times. Business Worth is established exclusively by Contagem or by Owner-Declared Business Worth (Decision 36, below) — never by Capital Inicial.
2. **Contagem means measurement, not a purchase, a stock-entry batch, or an accounting record of original investment.** An existing business may already have stock purchased long ago and partially sold or consumed by the time of its first Contagem under this model (e.g. `20 cx` originally purchased, `5 cx + 3 emb + 5 un` remaining at Contagem) — Contagem records what physically exists now, never a reconstruction of purchase history.
3. **Current Business Worth.** The existing Dashboard remains — this BDR does not redesign it. The existing "Capital Inicial" presentation is **conceptually replaced** by **Current Business Worth**: the Dashboard displays the latest confirmed/measured Business Worth. Estimated Business Worth is explicitly distinct from Current Business Worth (e.g. Current `620,000` vs. Estimated `645,000`) — the measured figure remains the official Current Business Worth until a new Contagem is confirmed, at which point the new measured value becomes Current Business Worth and the previous value becomes historical.
4. **Business Worth history.** Every confirmed Contagem under the new model creates a dated Business Worth snapshot. Snapshots are preserved, historical, immutable after the applicable correction/recovery windows close (Decisions 31–32), viewable, drillable, and never silently overwritten. Clicking the Business Worth card opens the Business Worth history, which must include the current record alongside every previous dated record (e.g. Current `22 Aug 2026 — 620,000`; History `10 Aug 2026 — 580,000`, `25 Jul 2026 — 540,000`). Clicking any date opens the complete information behind that measurement.
5. **Business Worth snapshot content.** A Business Worth record must let the owner understand "where did this value come from," drilling into: (1) product valuation total at Contagem; (2) product-level valuation detail; (3) embedded profit total; (4) batch-by-batch embedded profit detail; (5) cash position; (6) receivables position; (7) supplier/payable obligations; (8) expenses; (9) breakages; (10) Levantamentos; (11) other relevant contributing factors; (12) comparison/evolution against the previous Business Worth where applicable. Embedded profit must never be double-counted on top of a measured valuation where doing so would count the same economic value twice.
6. **Contagem product entry.** Physical quantities remain exactly in their entered units (e.g. `5 cx`, `3 emb`, `5 un`) — never forced into one displayed unit. Cost and selling prices remain displayed in their entered units (e.g. `2050 MZN/cx`, `100 MZN/un`). Internal conversion may occur only for calculation, never for display.
7. **Contagem selling-price flexibility.** Contagem supports both **Mode A** — one selling-unit price (e.g. `100 MZN/un`), internally converted across all physical quantities — and **Mode B** — multiple selling-unit prices simultaneously (e.g. `1200/cx`, `320/emb`, `60/un`), each applied to its own physical portion. The system internally calculates the total selling valuation in either mode; physical quantities and entered price units remain untouched. This multi-selling-unit flexibility applies to Contagem specifically.
8. **+Stock remains single-selling-unit.** The existing +Stock model is unchanged: one purchase unit, one cost unit, one selected selling unit, one selling price per batch. Product Memory may remember the latest prices. +Stock is not redesigned to support multiple simultaneous selling units merely because Contagem supports them (Decision 7).
9. **Cost/selling price memory.** During Contagem, the system may supply the latest known cost and the latest known selling price, and uses the product's own internal unit relationships for calculations. The owner should not repeatedly re-type information the system already knows, unless correcting or updating it according to the governed rules (`BDR-0012` Product Memory, §3 item 8).
10. **Contagem boundary.** The owner measures/verifies physical reality; the system supplies recorded financial reality. The system already knows or derives, from its own records: latest cost, latest selling price, unit relationships, embedded profits, expenses, breakages, Levantamentos, cash-ledger position, receivables, supplier obligations, and payment states. The owner should not repeatedly re-enter those values during every Contagem.
11. **Contagem reconciliation.** Contagem provides a reconciliation between physical/measured reality and system-recorded financial reality — for example, system cash `120,000` vs. physical cash `115,000`, a visible and explainable `-5,000` difference. The discrepancy must be visible and explainable; the system must never silently overwrite the ledger merely because a physical count differs. Contagem is therefore both a measurement and a reconciliation point.
12. **Cash.** Cash uses a **complete cash ledger** — it is not typed as a new arbitrary balance at every Contagem. The system maintains cash through recorded cash inflows/outflows: actual customer payments, supplier payments, expenses, Levantamentos, and other governed business cash movements, where applicable. Contagem may verify/reconcile physical cash against the ledger (Decision 11). The detailed technical storage design for the ledger is deferred to the Specification/Architecture stage (§6).
13. **Receivables.** Money owed to the business can be recorded. Unpaid receivables do **not** increase Business Worth (e.g. a `50,000` unpaid receivable leaves Business Worth unchanged). When the customer actually pays, the receivable decreases, cash increases, and the received amount becomes part of the business's actual financial position. Partial payments must be supported. The system distinguishes money owed from money actually received.
14. **Supplier obligations.** Recorded through stock/purchase activity, supporting Paid / Partially Paid / Unpaid states, each with total amount, amount paid, and amount remaining; payment events update the obligation. Outstanding supplier obligations reduce Business Worth. Paying an existing liability must **not** create a second, artificial Business Worth reduction on top of the liability already having reduced it.
15. **Supplier credit purchases.** A stock purchase made on credit does not itself increase Business Worth: the stock asset is matched by an equal liability (e.g. Current Business Worth `500,000`; purchase `25,000`; supplier liability `25,000`; embedded profit `5,000` → Estimated Business Worth `505,000`, not `530,000`). When the supplier is later paid, cash decreases and the liability decreases together — Business Worth does not artificially fall because of the payment itself. Business Worth measures economic position, not the internal conversion between cash, stock, and liabilities.
16. **Stock purchases using business money.** A purchase made using existing business cash (e.g. Current Business Worth `500,000`, owner uses `25,000` to buy stock with `5,000` embedded profit) is a conversion — cash → stock — not a new injection of Business Worth. Estimated Business Worth becomes `505,000`, not `530,000`; the growth component is the embedded profit.
17. **Expenses.** The existing Expense system and categories are kept — no new expense taxonomy is created merely because Business Worth is changing. Existing expense recording remains the source of truth. Expenses reduce Estimated Business Worth, without double-counting. Startup-phase spending may also contribute to Startup Investment reporting per the startup rules (Decision 20), without duplicating the underlying financial transaction.
18. **Breakages/Quebras.** The existing Quebra mechanism and valuation basis are kept unchanged — no redesign of the Quebra calculation merely because Business Worth is changing. The Business Worth model consumes existing Quebra records exactly per their established behavior (`07-breakages.md`).
19. **Levantamentos.** Money removed from the business: reduce the business's position because the money leaves; remain visible in business activity; are reported in Fecho; are **not** ordinary operating expenses; must not be double-counted.
20. **Startup Investment.** Records everything the owner spends until declaring "the business can stand on its own" — stock, materials, equipment, transport, labor, wages, preparation, licenses, and other legitimate startup expenditure. Must not duplicate +Stock purchases — if stock spending during startup already exists as a stock transaction, Startup Investment references/includes that investment for reporting rather than creating a second transaction. A large Startup Investment figure relative to a smaller first measured Business Worth (e.g. `800,000` vs. `310,000`) does not by itself indicate poor performance — these are different measurements.
21. **Estimated Business Worth.** Between confirmed Contagens, Estimated Business Worth begins from the latest measured Business Worth and reflects governed changes since that measurement: `Last Measured Business Worth + embedded profits − expenses − breakages − Levantamentos − applicable outstanding obligations/position changes`. The full cost of a stock purchase is never added when existing business money financed it (Decision 16); a supplier payment is never subtracted twice (Decision 15); a receivable is never treated as received money until actually paid (Decision 13). The exact technical implementation must preserve these economic principles — the formula's precise operationalization against live data structures is a Specification-stage concern (§6), not a reopened business question.
22. **Business Worth reconciliation.** When a new Contagem is performed, the system shows Previous Current Business Worth, Estimated Business Worth, New Measured Business Worth, and the Difference (e.g. Previous `500,000`, Estimated `550,000`, Measured `525,000`, Difference `-25,000`). The difference is a **reconciliation signal** — it is **not** automatically classified as expense, loss, theft, breakage, or error. The system helps the owner investigate possible causes rather than asserting a conclusion.
23. **New Contagem resets the estimate.** Once a new Contagem is confirmed, its measured value becomes Current Business Worth, and the next Estimated Business Worth period begins from that new measured value. Previous measurements remain historical. **(Logical consequence, converted to a formal rule):** this restates and formalizes, as its own explicit rule, exactly what Decisions 3–4 and 21 already imply together — no new business meaning is introduced by stating it separately.
24. **Fecho.** Reports (1) embedded profits, (2) Levantamentos, (3) expenses, (4) breakages, (5) Estimated Business Worth, over a flexible/custom owner-chosen date range (e.g. `02 August 2026 → 25 August 2026`). Levantamentos remain visible as activity, clearly marked as money removed from the business. This is a **DECIDED** business requirement (arbitrary date-range support, five required report lines); the exact technical mechanism for achieving arbitrary ranges against the existing `monthly`/`yearly`-only Fecho machinery is a Specification/Architecture question (§6), not decided here.
25. **Historical Capital Inicial / existing businesses.** Historical Capital Inicial values remain untouched — never deleted, rewritten, or fabricated. Existing businesses may use their preserved Capital Inicial as the starting baseline for Estimated Business Worth during transition (e.g. historical Capital Inicial `500,000`, then newly recorded activity contributes to Estimated Business Worth). A later new Contagem establishes the first Current Business Worth snapshot under the new model for that business. Old historical Contagens are never treated as though they contained cash, receivables, payables, or other data that was never actually collected.
26. **Historical data / transition.** Historical records remain historically valid. The new model applies prospectively to new information and new Business Worth measurements. No destructive migration and no fabrication of historical financial-position data. Existing Capital Inicial values are preserved. No old record is silently reinterpreted beyond the explicitly approved transition rule in Decision 25.
27. **Dashboard.** The existing Dashboard is kept — not redesigned. The existing Capital Inicial presentation becomes the Current Business Worth presentation under the new model, conceptually (Decision 3). Clicking the Business Worth card opens the Business Worth history/details (Decision 4).
28. **Business Worth history UI.** Contains the current record, previous dated records, and drill-down into each record. Clicking the current or any historical date opens the underlying information. Final historical records are read-only.
29. **Contagem autosave.** Contagem must autosave draft progress, protecting against leaving the screen, power loss, phone battery failure, accidental navigation, app/browser closure, and connection interruption. Autosave is **not** confirmation — draft data remains a draft until deliberate confirmation.
30. **Confirmation safety.** The final confirmation control must be safely separated/protected enough to reduce accidental confirmation. The owner must understand that confirmation finalizes the current measurement.
31. **Owner 3-hour correction window.** After Contagem confirmation, the Owner has exactly a **3-hour** correction window, timed from confirmation and not reset by later clicks. During this window the Owner can return and correct the Contagem. This window is structurally the same *kind* of mechanism `BDR-0015`'s Void & Redo already establishes for Initial Stock (a narrow, bounded exception to immutability, not a precedent for "immutable, except when..."), while remaining its own distinct figure for Contagem-derived Business Worth snapshots — it does not amend Void & Redo's own 12-hour Initial-Stock-specific window (§9).
32. **SuperAdmin recovery.** After the Owner's 3-hour correction window closes, SuperAdmin support may authorize recovery within a permitted **72-hour** ceiling. SuperAdmin does **not** directly edit the Owner's Contagem — the flow is: SuperAdmin authorizes → Owner performs recovery/edit → Owner confirms. After 72 hours, SuperAdmin cannot authorize recovery through this mechanism. The existing Confirmation #4 ceiling (`BDR-0015`/`POL-0008` Rule J) and other already-governed Initial Stock recovery ceilings remain unless explicitly amended later — this is a parallel, Contagem-derived-Business-Worth-specific boundary, not an amendment to `BDR-0015`/`BDR-0016`'s own governed figures.
33. **Immutability.** Outside the authorized correction/recovery windows, Business Worth snapshots and finalized historical Contagem data are viewable, drillable, immutable, and non-deletable through ordinary UI. Recovery is a controlled exception, never general editing authority.
34. **Architecture override.** The existing architecture deliberately excluded cash/receivables/payables/accounting-adjacent concepts. The Product Architect has explicitly decided these are required as **narrow inputs** to Business Worth and business decision-making — recorded as an explicit, narrow architecture-boundary override. This does **not** authorize POS, checkout, invoicing, payroll, full accounting, ERP, or general customer transaction management. Only the minimum financial-position information necessary for Business Worth is included.
35. **One umbrella governance model.** All of this belongs to **one** umbrella Business Worth evolution BDR. No separate BDR is created for cash, receivables, payables, startup investment, Business Worth history, Contagem, Fecho, estimated worth, or reconciliation. Later governance proceeds as one umbrella BDR (this document) → one Policy/governance chain → one consolidated Specification → one Rule 8 Assessment → one Implementation Plan → one Implementation Authorization. Implementation may still occur incrementally/feature-by-feature under that one umbrella authorization.
36. **Owner-Declared Business Worth [Product Architect Decision, 23 August 2026 — SIGNED, per the Governance Amendment Revision 3; reverses the establishment-mechanism portion of Gap 2's original resolution, §4 preamble above].** In addition to Contagem, the system supports a second, equally legitimate Business Worth establishment method: **Owner-Declared Business Worth.** An Owner who already knows their business's current worth (e.g., "I know my business is worth 350,000 MZN") establishes that figure directly, without a physical Contagem. This is not a shortcut around establishment or a lesser form of it — it produces a genuine `BusinessWorthSnapshot`, carries the same governance weight, and is never called Capital Inicial, Initial Capital, Estimated Capital, or Startup Investment. It is Business Worth, full stop, distinguished from Contagem-derived Business Worth only by its `establishmentMethod` provenance field (Specification §42/§8). There are exactly two establishment methods; no third exists or is introduced by any other part of this BDR or its amendments.

    **[Qualified, 23 August 2026, Product Architect Decision — Owner-Declared Verification Status.] "Same governance weight" and "It is Business Worth, full stop," above, are qualified as follows and must not be read more broadly than this qualification permits.** These phrases refer strictly to **procedural** treatment — correction eligibility, SuperAdmin-authorized recovery, auditability, immutability, and snapshot-lifecycle mechanics (supersession, correction/recovery windows) — all of which genuinely apply identically to a snapshot of either establishment method, unchanged by this qualification. **They do not, and never did, mean that SABUSH BPT has independently measured or verified an Owner-Declared figure.** An Owner-Declared value is a **claim** the Owner asserts, recorded and treated with full procedural weight, but never presented to the Owner or any other reader as though the system itself confirmed it. A confirmed Contagem remains the system's own independent, physical measurement — the only establishment method that produces a figure the system can honestly call **measured** or **verified**. This qualification changes no schema, no calculation, and no procedural mechanic — it corrects only how the *un-qualified* "same weight"/"full stop" language above could be misread as also claiming numerical-verification equivalence, which was never the intent and is not the effect of this Decision. See Specification §42.1 (as amended) for the corresponding display/terminology requirement this qualification introduces.

37. **First-Time Contagem Product-Information Model [Product Architect Decision, 23 August 2026 — APPROVED AND SIGNED, SABUSHIMIKE Masceni. Recorded here per this document's own §4 discipline; approved for future implementation only — this Decision does not itself authorize implementation; see the recording note immediately below the list].** When a genuinely new product is introduced through Contagem, the system must collect that product's complete information in one coherent first-time flow, structured as follows:

    a. **One product.** A product's remaining physical stock may be recorded across multiple portions/units in a single Contagem (e.g. Coca-Cola: 2 CX, 3 EMB, 5 UN). These are multiple portions of one product, never separate products, and the Owner must not be forced to repeatedly retype the same product name merely to record a different physical unit.

    b. **One original cost basis.** A product has exactly one original purchase unit and one original purchase cost (e.g. purchase unit CX, purchase cost 1,250 MZN/CX). The Owner never enters a separate cost per counted unit (no independent CX/EMB/UN cost prices) — the cost basis belongs to the product, not to each physical portion.

    c. **One full unit relationship.** The product's unit relationship may contain multiple chain levels (e.g. 1 CX = 4 EMB, 1 EMB = 6 UN, therefore 1 CX = 4 EMB = 24 UN), and the first-time Contagem flow must be capable of collecting the complete chain, not merely a single conversion step. The existing unit-conversion engine and `Product.unitRelationship` data model are reused exactly as they already exist; this Decision introduces no second conversion system and no new field.

    d. **Multiple current stock portions.** The physical stock counted in the current Contagem (e.g. 2 CX, 3 EMB, 5 UN) remains individually represented, each portion still belonging visibly to the same product. The Owner has a clear, product-level, first-class ability to add another portion/unit of the same product, without using a generic "product not in catalogue" action and retyping the product name.

    e. **Total Cost Value is calculated automatically, once.** Having entered the cost basis once, the system silently converts the counted portions into the original purchase-unit equivalent using the confirmed unit relationship and calculates the total (e.g. 2 + 3/4 + 5/24 = 2.958333... CX-equivalent × 1,250 MZN ≈ 3,697.92 MZN). Any per-unit rate implied by this conversion (e.g. 312.50 MZN/EMB) is, at most, an internal calculation intermediate — never stored, displayed, or treated as an independent product cost.

    f. **Selling valuation remains independent.** Selling units and selling prices are not the cost basis and are never derived from it. The Owner may set different selling units/prices per portion (e.g. 2 CX × 1,250, 3 EMB × 320, 5 UN × 60), exactly as the existing independent-portion and Mode A/Mode B selling behavior (Specification §15/FR-20, already authorized) already allows — this Decision changes nothing about that mechanism and does not make selling price depend on cost.

    g. **Total Selling Valuation is calculated automatically** from the current portions and their selling prices (e.g. 2,500 + 960 + 300 = 3,760 MZN), displayed to the Owner alongside, and visibly distinct from, Total Cost Value.

    h. **Business Worth continues to use the Total Selling Valuation, never the Total Cost Value.** In the worked example, Business Worth from this Contagem is 3,760.00 MZN, not 3,697.92 MZN. This Decision does not touch, weaken, or re-derive the existing selling-basis Business Worth path (`productValuationTotal`, `measuredBusinessWorth`) already established for Contagem.

    i. **First-time vs. subsequent Contagem.** For a genuinely new product, the first-time flow collects: product name, original purchase unit, original purchase cost, the full unit relationship, current physical portions, and current selling units/prices. Once a product's cost basis and unit relationship are established, subsequent Contagens reuse them automatically from Product Memory — the Owner is not asked to re-supply information the product already carries, and only enters current quantities/portions and current selling valuation.

    j. **UI/UX principle.** The first-time and reuse flows above must be presented as a coherent product-level card/group — one product, one cost basis, one unit relationship, many current portions, independent selling valuation, system-calculated totals — rather than a flat collection of unrelated rows. The existing grouped Initial Stock UX pattern (`groupRowsByProductName`, `RowGroup`, the "+ Adicionar Porção" affordance already shipped in `InitialStockCountView.tsx`) may be reused as UI precedent where appropriate; this Decision does not mandate a specific component implementation, only the model it must express.

    **What this Decision settles, and what it leaves open.** This Decision settles the *business meaning* of first-time Contagem product-information collection — the model in (a)–(j) above is DECIDED, in the same sense every other numbered item in this section is DECIDED. It settles nothing about exact schema, storage mechanism, exact UI component structure, or rounding precision beyond what is already fixed elsewhere (those remain Specification/Rule 8/Implementation-stage questions, per this document's own Type note, above). This Decision also does not, by itself, reopen, duplicate, or re-decide the cost-basis conversion arithmetic already settled and signed as Specification §15/FR-67 (Contagem Cost-Basis Conversion, Implementation Authorization §22 item 5/§25) or the selling-price independence/Mode A/Mode B behavior already settled and signed as Specification §15/FR-20 (Implementation Authorization, Increment 4) — items (b)/(e) and (f)/(g) above restate those already-governed principles for completeness within this one coherent model, they do not re-author them. The genuinely new content of this Decision, not previously recorded anywhere in this governance chain, is items (c) (arbitrary-length first-time chain collection), (d) (first-class "add portion" UI action, replacing the generic "product not in catalogue" workaround for this case), (i) (the explicit first-time-vs-subsequent-reuse rule for Contagem specifically), and (j) (the product-level-card UI principle for Contagem).

    **Recording note — this Decision does not authorize implementation.** Per this repository's established sequence (`Business Philosophy → BDR → Policy → Module Specifications → Rule 8 → Implementation`, §3/`19-governance-bdr-policy-framework.md` §3, the same sequence Decision 36 above followed), this Decision is the business-decision-layer record only. Before any code, test, `firestore.rules`, or `firestore.indexes.json` change may be made in furtherance of this Decision, the genuinely-new items identified above ((c), (d), (i), (j)) still require their own Rule 8 Assessment Addendum, Implementation Plan Amendment, and signed Implementation Authorization item — mirroring exactly how Decision 36 itself was carried through the Rule 8 Assessment Addendum (Revision 3), Implementation Plan Amendment (Revision 3), and Implementation Authorization Amendment (Revision 3, §22 item 1/§23 item 1) before any implementation instruction was given. ## 8. Business Acceptance Criteria

1. Business Worth is never shown as a known, measured figure for a business that has not yet performed its first standing-on-its-own Contagem, and no separate "business is operational" control exists outside that Contagem confirmation itself (Decisions 1, 25–26).
2. A confirmed Contagem under the new model produces a new, dated, immutable Business Worth snapshot distinct from, and never overwriting, any prior snapshot; confirming a new Contagem always resets the Estimated Business Worth baseline to the new measured value (Decisions 2–4, 23).
3. Every Business Worth snapshot exposes, at minimum, the twelve content items §4 Decision 5 names, drillable to product- and batch-level detail, and the reconciliation signal from Decision 22 is never auto-classified as expense, loss, theft, breakage, or error.
4. Contagem preserves physical quantities and entered unit-labeled prices exactly as entered, converting only internally for valuation math; supports both single- and multiple-selling-unit pricing; supplies known cost/selling-price memory so the owner need not re-enter what the system already knows; and reconciles physical reality against system-recorded reality (cash and analogous positions) without silently overwriting the ledger (Decisions 6–11), without altering +Stock's existing single-selling-unit-per-batch model (Decision 8).
5. Cash is maintained as a complete ledger of recorded inflows/outflows, never an arbitrary typed balance, and is reconcilable — not silently overwritten — against a physical Contagem count (Decisions 11–12).
6. A receivable never counts toward Business Worth until its status transitions to Paid (partial payment supported); a payable's Paid/Partially Paid/Unpaid lifecycle is fully tracked without double-counting a payment against the same liability (Decisions 13–14).
7. A stock purchase — whether financed by existing business cash or made on supplier credit — is never counted as new Business Worth beyond its own embedded profit (Decisions 15–16).
8. Estimated Business Worth between Contagens follows exactly the formula in Decision 21, with no double-counted stock-purchase-cost term, no double-subtracted supplier payment, and no receivable counted before actual payment.
9. The existing Expense and Quebra systems, categories, and valuation bases are unchanged by this capability; both reduce Estimated Business Worth without double-counting against Embedded Profit or Startup Investment (Decisions 17–18).
10. Levantamentos remain visible in business activity and in Fecho, clearly marked as money removed from the business, and are never treated as an ordinary operating expense (Decision 19).
11. Startup Investment tracking never duplicates a stock purchase already recorded via +Stock (Decision 20).
12. Fecho supports an arbitrary owner-chosen date range and reports exactly embedded profits, Levantamentos, expenses, breakages, and Estimated Business Worth for that range (Decision 24).
13. No existing business's historical Capital Inicial value is deleted, rewritten, or fabricated by this capability at any implementation stage, and no historical Contagem is reinterpreted as though it contained cash/receivable/payable data it never actually recorded (Decisions 25–26).
14. The existing Dashboard is not restructured; the Business Worth card opens a history containing the current record and every prior dated record, each drillable, with final historical records read-only (Decisions 3–4, 27–28).
15. Contagem autosaves continuously; a draft never becomes a confirmed snapshot without deliberate owner confirmation, which is itself protected against accidental triggering (Decisions 29–30).
16. The Owner has exactly a 3-hour correction window from confirmation, not reset by activity; after it closes, only a SuperAdmin-authorized recovery (Owner-executed, within 72 hours) can reopen it; after 72 hours, no recovery path exists through this mechanism; the existing Confirmation #4 ceiling and other Initial-Stock-specific recovery protections are unaffected (Decisions 31–32).
17. A finalized historical Business Worth snapshot, outside its correction/recovery windows, is viewable and drillable forever, and never directly editable or deletable through ordinary UI (Decision 33).
18. No cash/receivable/payable capability introduced under the Architecture Boundary Override extends into POS, invoicing, checkout, payroll, full accounting, ERP, or general customer transaction management (Decision 34).
19. No technical architecture, schema, algorithm, or UI is committed anywhere in this document (Decision 35 and §7's "does not decide" list).

- **Historical Capital Inicial values, wherever `BDR-0014`'s dual-valuation-basis choice already applies, remain exactly as `BDR-0014` and `10-stock-counts.md` already govern them** — this BDR's Decisions 25–26 (§4) are additive (Capital Inicial as a *starting baseline* for Estimated Business Worth during transition; its conceptual Dashboard role transferring to Current Business Worth, Decisions 3, 27), never a reinterpretation of what that historical figure itself means or how it was computed.
- Where this BDR's new snapshot model and `BDR-0014`'s existing dual-valuation-basis choice interact technically (e.g. whether a new-model Contagem snapshot itself carries a cost/selling basis choice, or whether it always preserves both, as Decisions 6–7 already imply for Contagem specifically) is a Specification-level reconciliation, not decided in this BDR beyond what Decisions 6–7 already state.
- No formal amendment/supersession mechanism is invented for `BDR-0014` by this document, consistent with the Product Architect's own instruction not to invent one unless the repository's governance rules require it.
