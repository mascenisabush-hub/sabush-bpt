## 1. The Business Reality

Sabush BPT's central promise is answering "what is my business worth" — not merely "what did I pay for my inventory." Two concrete, evidenced gaps stand between the current implementation and that promise, both confirmed directly against `main` before this BDR was drafted:

**Gap 1 — a selling-basis valuation is computed in places, but never preserved or surfaced as a first-class output.** `Product.sellingPrice`/`StockCountItem.sellingPrice` are already recorded today. Periodic Contagem's own `tallyStockCountRows` already computes `totalSellingValue` internally — and then discards it; no consumer anywhere in the codebase reads it. Initial Stock has a narrower, separate mechanism (`calculateInitialStockCurrentValuation`) that computes a current selling valuation, but only for display inside the price-change-recording modal — never as a headline figure, never on Dashboard, never in Reports.

**Gap 2 — "Initial Capital" is unconditionally cost-basis, with no owner choice.** `10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md` Part 2 and `stockCount.ts`'s `normalizeStockCountItems` both establish this deliberately, not by omission. The Product Architect's worked example — a shop with 100 jeans, cost 300/unit, selling 500/unit, currently realizable at only 100/unit — illustrates that historical acquisition cost and current realizable value are genuinely different numbers business owners think about differently, and the platform today can express only one of them as "Initial Capital."

**Related, already-confirmed capability this decision builds on, not reopens:** B5 (`3f84886`) and B6 (`0a06838`) already established, under the signed Implementation Authorization for Increment B §16–17, that the same product may appear as multiple portions within one Initial Stock or Periodic Contagem count, each with its own unit and valuation price, correctly summed by the existing, unmodified data model. This BDR assumes that capability as settled ground, not as something it re-decides.

## 2. Conceptual Vocabulary — Fixed Terms for This Decision and Everything Built From It

The following distinctions, evidenced directly in the investigation this BDR depends on, must not be conflated anywhere in this BDR or anything drafted from it:

- **Initial Stock cost valuation** — the sum, across every portion of every product in the confirmed `initial` Stock Count, of `quantity × costPrice`. Today, this **is** `initialStockCount.totalValue`, frozen at confirmation.
- **Initial Stock selling valuation** — the sum, across every portion, of `quantity × sellingPrice` — the owner's own deliberately recorded valuation/selling price at the moment of the count, per §5 below. This figure **already has the underlying data to be computed today** (both `StockCountItem.sellingPrice` and, for multi-portion products, B5's own summation behavior), but is not currently preserved as a total anywhere.
- **Initial Capital** — whichever of the above two figures is treated as *the* headline, authoritative baseline figure — read today, unconditionally, as the cost valuation (`initialCapitalValue = initialStockCount.totalValue`). This BDR's Decision 2 (§3, below) is what makes this a **choice** rather than a constant.
- **"Selling price," per this platform's own existing, unaltered convention** (confirmed directly in `types.ts`'s own field comments for `Product.sellingPrice`/`StockBatch.sellingPrice`/`StockCountItem.sellingPrice`): the **owner's own intended/expected selling price** — never an independently assessed or verified market valuation. This BDR does not introduce, redefine, or imply any different meaning for the term. Where this document says "selling valuation" or "selling-price valuation," it means exactly this — owner-declared, not third-party-verified.
- **Product Memory** (`BDR-0012`) — the confirmed unit relationship and remembered selling unit/price for a product, across all future transactions. Distinct from, and never overwriting, an Initial Stock portion's own historically recorded quantity/unit/cost/selling price (§5, below).
- **Business Worth** (`02-business-worth-engine.md`) — the single authoritative, StockBatch-derived, all-time cumulative figure this BDR explicitly does not redefine (§7, below).

## 3. Decisions Formally Established

1. **Both valuation bases must be computed and preserved for Initial Stock — REQUIRED BUSINESS DECISION, substantially evidenced by already-approved governance.** When Initial Stock is confirmed, the system must retain, as historical fact of that snapshot, both the cost valuation (sum of `quantity × costPrice`, across all portions) and the selling valuation (sum of `quantity × sellingPrice`, across all portions). Neither figure may be discarded because the other is also recorded. This decision substantially restates, and closes an implementation gap against, `BDR-0009` Decision 7 (*"The system automatically calculates report values [per-product and total physical purchase value, per-product and total physical selling value]... No new financial figure beyond straightforward multiplication and summation is introduced"*) — that decision already anticipated both totals; today's implementation computes the selling total in one place (Periodic Contagem's `tallyStockCountRows`) and discards it, and computes a narrower current-selling-value figure for Initial Stock in a different, disconnected place (`calculateInitialStockCurrentValuation`, scoped only to the repricing modal). This Decision requires that the **total** selling valuation become a preserved, first-class fact of the confirmed Initial Stock snapshot itself — not merely an intermediate value inside an unrelated feature.

2. **The owner may choose which basis "Initial Capital" displays — REQUIRED BUSINESS DECISION, genuinely new, directly reconciled in §4.** Initial Capital supports an explicit valuation-basis choice: cost-based or selling-based. This is a **presentation/interpretation choice layered on top of** both preserved valuations (Decision 1) — **it is not authorization to stop computing or storing either basis, and it is not authorization to rewrite, replace, or unfreeze the historical `totalValue` figure `10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md` Part 2 already governs.** Both the underlying cost valuation and selling valuation remain recorded, permanently, regardless of which one the owner has chosen for display. Whether the choice applies retroactively, whether it is mutable, what `capitalGrowth` reads as a result, and what scope the choice has are each now explicitly resolved in §5.A, items 1–4, below (§5.A Resolution Log) — none was decided by inference or left to the eventual Specification to guess at, precisely because `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s own `capitalGrowth = businessWorth − initialCapitalValue` formula depends on part of this resolution (§7).

3. **Initial Stock is historical and immutable — DIRECTLY EVIDENCED, restated, not newly decided.** Once confirmed, a portion's quantity, unit, cost price, selling/valuation price, unit-relationship information, and both calculated valuation totals (cost and selling) are historical facts of that snapshot. A later Product Memory change, a later price-change event, or a later purchase of the same product at a different price must never rewrite them. This restates, and extends to the newly-preserved selling-valuation total, exactly what `BDR-0009` Decisions 7–8 and `10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md` Part 2 already establish for the cost-basis figure — this BDR does not weaken, reopen, or create any exception to that immutability discipline; it extends the identical discipline to a second, now-equally-historical figure.

4. **A later purchase is a new, independent StockBatch fact — DIRECTLY EVIDENCED, restated, not newly decided.** A later Add Stock purchase of the same product creates its own `StockBatch` document with its own purchase facts and, where Product Memory applies, its own currently-remembered selling price (`BDR-0012` Decisions 2–4, 15). It does not, and must not, retroactively alter the Initial Stock record for that product, in either basis. This BDR does not touch, and does not require touching, `StockBatch`, `addMultipleStockBatches`, or Product Memory's own write path.

5. **Multiple portions of one product remain one product — DIRECTLY EVIDENCED, restated, not newly decided.** A product may legitimately appear as multiple Initial Stock rows/portions, each with its own recorded unit, quantity, cost price, and valuation/selling price — the owner deliberately establishing that portion's stock value at that moment, not creating a duplicate product. This restates exactly what B5 (`3f84886`) already implements, under the already-signed Increment B Implementation Authorization's §16 scope, and this BDR's own Decisions 1–2 apply identically to each portion, individually and in sum — the selling-valuation total (Decision 1) is the sum across all of a product's portions, exactly as the cost-valuation total already is today.

6. **Product Memory informs interpretation; it never overwrites a portion's recorded facts — DIRECTLY EVIDENCED, restated, not newly decided.** Where a product has a confirmed unit relationship (e.g. `1 Cx = 24 Un`), the system may use that relationship to help the owner understand or reconcile physical quantities across portions, exactly as B5/B6 and the accepted UOM Specification already establish. It must never use that relationship to silently rewrite a portion's owner-entered quantity, unit, cost price, or valuation price. This restates `BDR-0012` Decisions 2–3 and 15, applied to Initial Stock's own recorded facts specifically.

7. **Periodic Contagem carries the identical valuation-basis principle — REQUIRED BUSINESS DECISION, narrower in scope than Decision 2.** The owner may deliberately value different portions of the same product at different valuation/selling prices appropriate to each portion at the time of a Periodic Contagem, exactly as Decision 1 establishes for Initial Stock, and the system must preserve and sum a total selling valuation for the count, alongside the existing cost-basis total, closing the same "computed then discarded" gap `tallyStockCountRows`'s own `totalSellingValue` currently has. **This Decision explicitly does NOT extend Decision 2's owner-chosen-basis concept to Periodic Contagem** — Periodic Contagem has no equivalent of "Initial Capital" as a single frozen headline figure requiring a basis choice; its existing comparison mechanism (`expectedCurrentStockValue`, cost-basis) is unaffected by this BDR and is not reopened here. **This Decision explicitly does not touch, redefine, or otherwise depend on Periodic Contagem's date/period model.** That subject was raised in the same governance session and has been explicitly and deliberately set aside — nothing in this BDR should be read as reopening, deciding, or assuming any outcome for it.

## 4. Explicit Reconciliation With Existing Approved Governance

**This section exists because Decision 2 directly conflicts with existing, currently-binding governance, and that conflict is not silently ignored, resolved by inference, or treated as already settled.**

1. **`10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md` Part 2 ("Initial Capital Integrity"):** *"Initial Capital is historical truth. The confirmed `initial` `StockCount` — and therefore `initialCapitalValue` (`initialStockCount.totalValue`) — is never rewritten, edited, or recalculated because of a later price-change event, regardless of how many events exist or what they record."* This is enforced today at the Security Rules layer (`firestore.rules`' `stockCounts` block refuses `update`/`delete` unconditionally for `type == 'initial'`).
   **This BDR does not conflict with the immutability half of this rule** (Decision 3, above, restates and extends it). **It did potentially conflict with the implicit assumption that `initialCapitalValue` denotes exactly one, permanently cost-basis figure** — Decision 2 introduces a basis *choice* for what "Initial Capital" displays. This is now reconciled: per §5.A items 1–2, the choice is prospective-only (an already-confirmed count is never reopened) and fixed once set (mirroring the same immutability discipline `10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md` Part 2 already applies elsewhere), so the frozen, historical character of an *already-confirmed* Initial Stock count's `totalValue` is fully preserved — no already-confirmed count's cost-basis figure ever becomes, or is ever at risk of becoming, a different number.
2. **`product-memory-purchase-selling-valuation-specification.md` §19 ("Initial Capital Implications — [SEPARATE GOVERNANCE REQUIRED]"):** this document explicitly anticipated and required exactly the governance action this BDR now performs: *"a selling-basis Initial Capital display, in any form, requires its own separate BDR (or a formal amendment to `10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md` and/or `10-stock-counts.md`), explicitly reconciling it against the 'Initial Capital is historical truth, cost-basis, frozen' rule those documents already establish."* **This BDR is that required separate BDR.** §19 further suggested, as one direction worth considering (not itself authorized there): *"A parallel, non-frozen, count-specific display of what §16's mixed-valuation portions would total under a selling-price lens — shown alongside, not replacing, the frozen cost-basis `totalValue` — may be a narrower and less conflicting way to satisfy the underlying business need."* This BDR's Decision 1 (both bases preserved, neither discarded) is consistent with that suggested direction; Decision 2 (an owner-chosen *display* basis) goes further than that suggestion by name — its reconciliation mechanism is now resolved per item 1, above, and formalized in `10-initial-stock-dual-valuation-basis-amendment.md` (Accepted).
3. **`stockCount.ts`'s `normalizeStockCountItems`, explicitly, deliberately cost-basis** (in-code comment: *"matching Expected Current Stock Value's existing cost-based rule"*): Decision 1 requires this function's scope to expand to also produce a preserved selling-valuation total — a genuine functional change to an existing, heavily-tested module, not merely a new consumer of its existing output. This is flagged here as a real implementation-scope fact for the eventual Specification/Rule 8 stage; this BDR does not itself change this function.
4. **`02-business-worth-engine.md`'s "Capital Growth" section:** *"`capitalGrowth = businessWorth − initialCapitalValue`."* Because `initialCapitalValue` is `capitalGrowth`'s own operand, Decision 2's basis choice has a direct, unavoidable ripple effect into this already-approved formula's *input*, even though this BDR does not touch `businessWorth` itself (§7, below) and `businessWorth`'s own formula contains no `initialCapitalValue` term at all (confirmed directly in the investigation this BDR depends on). **This is now resolved: per §5.A item 3, `capitalGrowth` follows the chosen basis** — the formula's shape is unchanged; only what `initialCapitalValue` may now resolve to (cost or selling, per that business's own fixed, prospective-only choice) changes. This resolution is formalized in a dedicated companion amendment, `02-capital-growth-dual-basis-amendment.md`, filed alongside this BDR — `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s "Explicit non-goal" clauses for the Expected-Stock-Value and Initial-Stock-Valuation-History amendments remain entirely unaffected and are not reopened by this BDR or its companion amendment.
5. **`10-stock-counts.md`'s existing description of Initial Capital as "the permanent baseline everything else is measured against"** (its own words, describing why the screen matters): unaffected in substance — Decision 2 does not change what Initial Capital *is for*, only what basis it may be expressed in, per the owner's choice.

**None of these five artifacts is modified by this BDR.** Each requires formal, explicit amendment — through this repository's own governance process — before implementation can proceed. **This BDR's approval alone did not authorize touching any of them; it only established the business decision such amendments would eventually need to implement.** Two companion amendments, formalizing items 1 and 4's now-resolved reconciliation, are filed alongside this BDR: `10-initial-stock-dual-valuation-basis-amendment.md` (reconciling `10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md`/`10-stock-counts.md`) and `02-capital-growth-dual-basis-amendment.md` (reconciling `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s Capital Growth section). **Both are now Accepted** (§9, §11) — neither edits its base document's own file; both remain, by design, governance reconciliation records rather than in-place edits.

## 5. Open Decisions, Classified by Governance Layer

### 5.A — Business/Philosophy Decisions

*(Questions affecting the business meaning or scope of this capability — must be resolved at the BDR/business-decision level before technical architecture proceeds. Originally filed as open; each item below is now RESOLVED, per explicit Product Architect ratification recorded in the §5.A Resolution Log immediately following.)*

1. **Retroactive vs. prospective application of the basis choice — RESOLVED: prospective only.** The basis choice applies only to an Initial Stock count confirmed from this capability's existence forward. An Initial Stock count already confirmed before this capability exists is not reopened, is not retroactively offered a choice, and continues exactly as it already does today — unconditionally cost-basis — because `10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md` Part 2's immutability rule (restated by this BDR's own Decision 3) already forbids reopening an already-confirmed, frozen count for any reason, including to add a field it was never confirmed with. No existing business's historical `capitalGrowth` figures are put at risk of changing as a side effect of this capability — a business with an already-confirmed Initial Stock count simply never sees this choice for that count.
2. **Mutability of the basis choice — RESOLVED: fixed once set.** Once chosen, at the moment of that Initial Stock count's confirmation, the basis is permanent — mirroring exactly the immutability discipline this codebase already applies to `costPrice` and every other historical financial fact (`BDR-0012` Decisions 15–16; this BDR's own Decision 3). No later toggle, no reversal, no audit-trail-of-changes mechanism is required, because there is nothing to audit — the choice is made once, as part of the same single confirmation action that already freezes every other fact of that count.
3. **What `capitalGrowth` (and any other existing consumer of `initialCapitalValue`) reads once a basis choice exists — RESOLVED: `capitalGrowth` follows the chosen basis.** `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s formula, `capitalGrowth = businessWorth − initialCapitalValue`, is **unchanged in shape**. What changes is that `initialCapitalValue` — the value that formula's second operand already was and remains — now resolves to whichever basis (cost or selling) was chosen and frozen for that business's Initial Stock, per items 1–2 above, rather than being unconditionally the cost-basis total as it is today. This is a **deliberate, explicit decision that `capitalGrowth`'s meaning may now vary by business**, made openly here — not a silent consequence the implementation would otherwise have had to guess at. A dedicated companion amendment to `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s "Capital Growth" section, reflecting this exact resolution, is required before this item can be treated as implementation-ready — see `02-capital-growth-dual-basis-amendment.md`, filed alongside this BDR.
4. **Scope of "the owner's choice" — RESOLVED: a field on the Initial Stock record itself, not a separate business-level setting.** Because Initial Stock is already a singleton per business (`firestore.rules` refuses a second `type: 'initial'` count unconditionally), this is operationally equivalent to a business-level setting today — but it is conceptually and structurally anchored to the Initial Stock record's own confirmation, not a freestanding business preference that could exist independent of that record, and not a setting that could apply to Periodic Contagem or any other future valuation event without its own separate decision (Decision 7 already excludes Periodic Contagem from this capability entirely).

**§5.A Resolution Log** — Items 1, 2, and 4 were resolved via explicit Product Architect single-select decisions, recorded in this project's governance session, in the order: item 4 (scope), item 1 (retroactivity), item 2 (mutability). Item 3 (`capitalGrowth`'s meaning) was resolved separately, immediately after, once the dependency those three answers created was made explicit — the Product Architect explicitly declined to let this be inferred silently, consistent with this BDR's own §4 item 4 flag and the explicit instruction that this dependency "should be resolved explicitly rather than allowing the selling-basis Initial Capital choice to silently change an existing metric." All four items are now fully resolved decisions of this BDR, not merely recommendations. No item in §5.A remains open.

## 6. What This BDR Decides, and What It Does Not

**Decides:**
- That both cost and selling valuation totals must be computed and preserved as historical fact of a confirmed Initial Stock snapshot (Decision 1).
- That Initial Capital may support an owner-chosen display basis, without ever discarding either underlying valuation or unfreezing the historical record (Decision 2).
- That Initial Stock's historical immutability, Product Memory's non-overwriting boundary, and B5/B6's multi-portion capability all extend cleanly to this dual-valuation concept, restating rather than reopening prior decisions (Decisions 3–6).
- That Periodic Contagem carries the same preserve-both-totals principle, narrower than Decision 2, and explicitly without touching its date/period model (Decision 7).
- That Decision 2 conflicts with `10-initial-stock-valuation-history-amendment.md` Part 2's implicit single-basis assumption, now reconciled per §5.A items 1–2 (prospective-only, fixed-once-set), and has a direct, now-explicitly-resolved effect on `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s `capitalGrowth` formula's input, per §5.A item 3 and the companion `02-capital-growth-dual-basis-amendment.md` (§4).

**Does not decide:**
- Any technical data model, schema, or Firestore field.
- Any UI/interaction design.
- Any rounding/precision rule beyond the existing `POL-0001`/`POL-0002` convention.
- Anything about Business Worth's own formula, which remains exactly as `02-business-worth-engine.md` already defines it — **this BDR does not redefine, and does not authorize anyone else to silently redefine, how Business Worth combines Initial Stock, StockBatch inventory, expenses, withdrawals, or realized/period profit.** That broader question remains a distinct, future Product Architect decision, entirely separate from this one.
- Anything about Periodic Contagem's date/period model — explicitly and deliberately excluded (Decision 7).
- A migration or backfill strategy for any historical Initial Stock count recorded before this capability exists.

## 7. Explicit Non-Goal — Business Worth Is Not Defined Here

**Restated with maximum clarity, because this is the single most important boundary of this BDR:** this document establishes *how Initial Stock and Initial Capital are valued and displayed*. It does **not** establish *how the final Business Worth formula must combine Initial Stock, StockBatch inventory, expenses, withdrawals, realized profit, or any other value pool*. The investigation this BDR depends on confirmed that `businessWorth`'s current formula (`AppContext.tsx:822`) contains no `initialCapitalValue` term at all — Initial Stock, in either basis, does not enter it today, and this BDR does not change that. §4 item 4/§5.A item 3 resolve the one real, narrow point of contact (`capitalGrowth`'s own operand) explicitly, by name, rather than silently — **`businessWorth` itself remains completely untouched by that resolution.** Any future work combining these value pools requires its own, separate Product Architect decision, on its own terms.

## 8. Business Acceptance Criteria

1. A confirmed Initial Stock snapshot's cost valuation and selling valuation are both computed and preserved, for every portion and in total, and neither is discarded because the other exists (Decision 1).
2. Initial Capital supports an owner-chosen display basis without ever ceasing to preserve, or unfreezing, either underlying valuation (Decision 2).
3. No historically confirmed Initial Stock fact — quantity, unit, cost price, selling price, unit-relationship information, or either valuation total — is rewritten by a later Product Memory change, price-change event, or purchase (Decision 3).
4. A later purchase of the same product creates its own independent `StockBatch` fact and never retroactively alters the Initial Stock record (Decision 4).
5. Multiple portions of one product remain one product, correctly summed in both bases (Decisions 1, 5).
6. Product Memory informs interpretation of quantities across portions but never silently overwrites a portion's owner-entered facts (Decision 6).
7. Periodic Contagem preserves both valuation totals per the same principle, without gaining an "Initial Capital"-style basis choice and without any change to its date/period model (Decision 7).
8. `02-business-worth-engine.md`'s `businessWorth` formula is unmodified; `capitalGrowth`'s dependency on `initialCapitalValue` is explicitly resolved, by name, per §5.A item 3 — never silently (§4 item 4, §7).
9. An already-confirmed Initial Stock count is never reopened, and its historical `capitalGrowth` figures are never put at risk of changing, as a side effect of this capability (§5.A items 1–2).
10. No technical architecture, schema, algorithm, or UI is committed anywhere in this document.
11. No historical migration or backfill is authorized.
